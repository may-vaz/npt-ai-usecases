What are the best AI use cases for cutting non-productive time in drilling and well delivery?

Five use cases, each defined as: a specific delay, the data behind it, what the AI does, who acts on it, and how the result is measured.

Not every AI idea belongs on this list

Selection criteria

Each candidate use case was tested against five criteria before making the cut; the same lens is used to rank them further down the page.

01

NPT impact

Does it target a documented, sizeable share of lost time, not a marginal edge case?

02

Data readiness

Does the required data already exist in a usable form, or does it need new instrumentation first?

03

Time to value

Can it produce a measurable result in one drilling season, not a multi-year build?

04

Safety & explainability

Can every output be traced to evidence, with a human making the final call?

05

Ease of adoption

Does it fit how the driller, engineer or planner already works, or does it demand a new workflow?

The five use cases

Each one follows the same structure: the operational problem, what the AI actually does, who acts on the output, what action it enables, and how success is measured, followed by the real evidence behind the numbers.

01
Pre-spud offset-well intelligence
High priority
Operational problem
Engineers spend a disproportionate share of well-planning time searching, cross-checking and reformatting old reports instead of deciding, most of it going into offset-well analysis rather than judgment.
AI intervention
Reads historical end-of-well reports and daily reports, structures them, and surfaces comparable offset wells, past risks and mitigations that actually worked.
Human user
Drilling / well engineer during well planning.
Action enabled
The plan is amended, or a contingency is added, before spud, not discovered mid-well.
Data required
End-of-well reports, daily reports, well logs, historical risk registers.
Success measure
Planning time saved · number of relevant historical lessons surfaced · risks caught before spud.

Evidence

>40%of engineers' time spent searching & validating, not deciding
5sto retrieve a ranked offset-well shortlist via vector search
Sources: Baker Hughes JewelSuite Well Engineering (offset-well search reduced from 5 days to 5 hours per project); Vertechs field note, 2026 ("engineers often spend over 40% of their time searching, cross-checking and validating information"); AADE-26-FTCE-102 (Morrison): offset-well vector search returned in under 5 seconds.
02
Real-time early warning during drilling
High priority
Operational problem
Stuck pipe, pack-off, poor hole cleaning and losses often only become obvious once the situation has already deteriorated.
AI intervention
Analyses live torque, drag, ROP, pressure and hole-depth data and flags combinations of signals matching historical problem patterns.
Human user
Driller, drilling engineer and mud engineer.
Action enabled
The team investigates and applies the approved operating procedure while there is still time to respond. The AI never controls the rig; it flags, the crew decides.
Data required
Torque, drag, ROP, standpipe pressure, flow, hole depth, mud properties, historical stuck-pipe/kick event records.
Success measure
Warning lead time · false-alert rate · number of alerts acted on successfully · avoided NPT hours.

Evidence

1.5%false-positive drilling hours in field-validated model
F1 ≈ 0.75event-level detection score, same field trial
Sources: EAGE hybrid stuck-pipe workflow, field-validated on Asian wells, 2026 (all major events alerted, 19-hour average lead time, 1.5% false-positive drilling hours, F1 ≈ 0.75); Salminen et al., SPE-178888-PA, 2017 (Weatherford SPR method: 38-minute average lead time on shale wells, 2+ hours on a carbonate well, zero false alerts across the historical test set).
03
Daily lookahead & constraint management
Medium priority
Operational problem
Time is lost waiting on equipment, crew, approvals or materials that weren't ready when the schedule needed them: a logistics gap, not a subsurface one.
AI intervention
Continuously compares the well plan against actual progress, open actions, and resource/logistics constraints to flag a likely delay before it reaches the critical path.
Human user
Drilling superintendent / planner.
Action enabled
Re-sequence activities, expedite an approval, or reposition equipment ahead of the conflict, instead of discovering it on the day.
Data required
Well plan, execution status, equipment/crew availability, approval-workflow data.
Success measure
Waiting hours avoided · schedule variance · overdue actions closed before impact.

Evidence

99%less time to generate an optimal schedule vs. manual methods
11–24%reduction in rig mobilisation distance / fuel burned
Source: "Optimizing Rig Scheduling Through AI," SPE, 2022: constraint-based (Knapsack/VRP) AI scheduling benchmarked against conventional rig-scheduling methods across in-house case studies. The same study reports a trade-off worth weighing alongside the gains above: a 5% reduction in asset utilisation.
04
NPT classification & root-cause analysis
High priority
Operational problem
NPT is recorded in free-text daily reports, worded differently by every engineer and every rig, making it impossible to benchmark or spot a recurring cause at scale.
AI intervention
Reads every daily report, extracts NPT event start/end times, classifies each against one standard taxonomy, and rolls the results up across wells and contractors.
Human user
Drilling engineer / performance analyst.
Action enabled
The company addresses the specific recurring cause (a procedure, a contractor, a formation) instead of re-diagnosing it well by well.
Data required
Daily drilling reports (free text), historical NPT logs, contractor/service-line records.
Success measure
Classification accuracy · time to prepare an NPT analysis · reduction in repeat NPT events.

Evidence

$4.2Msaved across a 100-well campaign, one AI-DDR deployment
30%increase in drilling-engineer productivity, same deployment
Sources: Provectus / US upstream operator case study, 2026 (weeks of manual DDR review cut to hours; $4.2M saved and one month of rig time recovered across a 100-well campaign; 30% productivity increase); JPME 2024, 26-rig NPT-coding study (overall NPT 24.9% → 17.7%; contractor-related NPT 11.2% → 5.4%; ~$40M saved per rig set annually).
05
Equipment failure prediction
Lower priority, for now
Operational problem
Unplanned failure of critical equipment, such as mud pumps or top drives, stops the entire operation with no warning.
AI intervention
Monitors vibration, pressure and performance trends against normal operating signatures to flag degradation before failure.
Human user
Rig maintenance lead.
Action enabled
Repair is scheduled during a planned pause (a trip, a connection) instead of an unplanned stoppage.
Data required
Continuous vibration/condition sensors, maintenance history, equipment performance logs. This is the main constraint on this use case.
Success measure
Downtime avoided · unplanned-failure rate · maintenance cost vs. downtime cost avoided.

Evidence

$320kin equipment cost & downtime avoided, one 5-month pilot
27 days/yraverage unplanned downtime an offshore rig otherwise carries
Sources: Precision Drilling Digital Twin case study, 2025 (proactive mobilisation on a suspected traction-motor failure cut downtime by >50%); KCF Technologies / RigCLOUD, Nabors pilot (4 rigs, 96 sensors, 5 months: $320,000 in avoided equipment cost and downtime); ACOEM offshore case study (20% reduction in unplanned downtime, 15% lower maintenance cost); Kimberlite, via Avathon (average offshore rig: 27 unplanned downtime days/year, ~$38M in losses).
One layer underneath all five

How this would actually get built

The five use cases above don't need five separate systems. They share one pipeline, with a different specialised agent doing the work at each stage, and a human approving the output every time.

1

Aggregate

Daily reports, end-of-well reports and, where available, live rig telemetry are pulled into one place and cleaned.

→
2

Specialised agents

One agent per job (offset-well retrieval, anomaly detection, schedule-constraint checking, NPT classification, condition monitoring), each narrow enough to validate on its own.

→
3

Human sign-off

Every flag, plan change or classification is traced back to its source document or sensor reading before the accountable person acts on it.

→
4

Closed-loop learning

What actually happened after each decision (right call, false alarm, missed event) feeds back in, so the next well starts smarter than the last.

This is a build-order, not a feature list: stage 1–2 needs only the documents already on file (use cases #1 and #4); stage 2's real-time agents (#2) need a live rig data connection first; stage 4 only becomes possible once enough wells have been through the loop to learn from.

Where to begin

Two of these five already run on data that exists today

Pre-spud offset-well intelligence and NPT classification (#1 and #4) are placed first because they run on data that already exists today, daily reports and end-of-well reports, with nothing new to instrument or connect.. Real-time early warning (#2) addresses the single largest share of NPT of any use case here, but it depends on a live data connection to the rig rather than documents alone, which is a different kind of undertaking. Daily lookahead (#3) and equipment failure prediction (#5) are just as real and just as evidenced, but each depends on a data feed, logistics status for one, continuous sensor data for the other, that isn't uniformly in place across every rig yet.

None of this is a judgment on which use case matters most; it's a reflection of what each one needs before it can run. In short, the best use case is the one that helps a team prevent a problem, catch it earlier, recover faster, or stop repeating it on the next well, and right now, that starting point is sitting in the reports already on file.
